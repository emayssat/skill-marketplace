# Single Persona Reaction (Quick Read)

**When to use:** Someone asks a one-persona question — "how would a CTO react to this?", "would an SRE buy this claim?", "does this land for a teenager?" — against any material at any maturity: an idea, a script, a few frames, a finished cut. Minutes, not a review round.

This is the lightest workflow here. Use it freely; it's the everyday one.

**Inputs required:** The material, and which persona to ask. That's it.

**Pace:** a quick way to sanity-check a read before committing to it — ask Nadia Language or Cora Cognitive whether a beat is followable at the pace it's written. Cheaper than a preview round, and it catches the obvious cases.

## Sequence

| # | Persona | Task | Output / gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | producer | Name the persona, the material, and the actual question being asked | Framed question |
| 2 | *the named persona* | React in character — their priorities, their drop-off triggers, their voice — then answer the specific question, staying inside their "Not their concern" boundary | In-character reaction |
| 3 | director | Sanity-check scope: was this the right persona to ask, and is the reaction inside their lane? | Scope check |

## Final output

An in-character reaction. If it's material to a production, drop it into that production's `feedback/` folder rather than losing it in chat.

## Notes / anti-patterns

- **One persona.** If you want several, that's a **Preview** — and a preview has a prepared cut and a real panel for a reason. Stacking five quick reads is not the same thing and produces averaged mush.
- A single persona's reaction is a data point, not a verdict. Don't let "Camille Chief wouldn't like it" become a decision by itself.
- Respect the boundary in step 2. Asked about shot composition, Theo Teen has nothing useful to say, and inventing an opinion for him makes every other persona's feedback less trustworthy. "That's not something this persona would judge" is a valid answer.
- Asking the flattering persona is self-deception. If the question is whether a claim holds up, ask Simone Skeptic or Silas Security, not the persona already inclined to agree.
- For a fast bias check, run an opposing disposition pair instead of one persona: **Olive Optimist and Pia Pessimist** on the same material, or **Frank Facts and Esme Emotion**. Two opposed reads cost barely more than one and tell you something a single read can't — whether the piece is actually persuasive or just agreeable to whoever you happened to ask.
