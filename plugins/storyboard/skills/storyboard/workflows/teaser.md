# Teaser Storyboard

**When to use:** You need a very short anticipation-builder — typically 10 to 30 seconds — released well ahead of the main piece, designed to signal that something is coming without explaining what it is. Distinct from **Trailer**: a trailer is mined from a finished or fully boarded piece and shows enough to sell it; a teaser can be built from a single hero moment before the full piece even exists, and deliberately withholds almost everything.

**Inputs required:** At minimum, a locked concept and one visual or idea strong enough to carry the whole teaser. The full piece does *not* need to be finished — but the teased moment must be one you're confident survives into the final cut.

**Pace:** a teaser is words-poor by design — often one line. Density is rarely the risk; the opposite is. If a 15-second teaser needs 40 words to make sense, the concept is too complicated for the format, and that's a step-2 finding rather than something to solve by talking faster.

**Protected cuts:** read that standing note **before** looking for anything to lose. Logan Legal's cleared claims, Sonny Sound's consented testimonials, Claude Client's value-proposition beat and Arthur Art's brand minimums are all in there, and they are exactly the frames that look cheapest to cut. To change one, go back to the persona who protected it — not to the producer, not to the director. `scripts/boards.py --notes` fails on a protected frame marked CUT.

**Cards:** a teaser's cards are copied from the source piece, and what gets copied is the **current state only** — not the source board's Revision log, not its Still open issues, not a Note explaining why the source frame changed last spring. Those belong to that board. This one starts its own history at v1.

## Sequence

| # | Persona | Task | Output / gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | producer | Define teaser length, placement, and release date relative to the main piece | Teaser brief |
| 2 | director | Choose the single moment or idea the teaser hangs on, and decide explicitly what must *not* be revealed | Teaser concept + no-reveal list |
| 3 | scriptwriter | Write the minimal copy — often one line plus a title card | Teaser copy |
| 4 | storyboard-artist | Board the handful of frames needed; these may be new rather than pulled from the source piece | Teaser frames |
| 5 | art-director | Ensure the title card and logo treatment establish the campaign look the later pieces will inherit | Approved graphics |
| 6 | editor | Assemble at teaser pace — fast, withholding, ending before the viewer is satisfied | Teaser cut |
| 7 | sound-designer | Plant the sonic motif or sting that the trailer and full piece will later pay off; at this length audio carries disproportionate weight | Teaser mix |
| 8 | producer | Confirm delivery specs and release timing relative to the full piece | Confirmed specs + date |
| 9 | client | Approve on curiosity generated, not on comprehension | Approved teaser |

## Final output

A standalone teaser cut, plus the visual and sonic motifs established here for the trailer and full piece to pay off later.

## Notes / anti-patterns

- A teaser that explains the product has quietly become a weak trailer. The step-2 gate is "does this make someone want more," never "does this tell them what it is."
- This is where the motif gets planted — the sound sting (step 7) and title treatment (step 5) should be deliberately reusable, not one-offs.
- Don't tease a moment from an unlocked cut. If the teased shot gets cut from the final piece later, the teaser retroactively becomes a promise you didn't keep.
- If the piece you're promoting is already finished and you want to show real substance from it, that's the **Trailer** workflow, not this one.
