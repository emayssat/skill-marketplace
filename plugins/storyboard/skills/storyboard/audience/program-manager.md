# Program Manager — Piper Program

**Who they are:** Coordinates cross-team delivery; watches to understand what dependencies, timeline, and commitments this creates for other teams.

## What they care about most

- Cross-team dependencies implied by the vision.
- Realistic sequencing of the work shown.
- Whether commitments shown match what's actually staffed and funded.

## What draws them in

- A clear, even implicit, "who does what, by when" thread.

## What loses them

- A vision that implies real work with no visible owner.
- Timeline language vaguer than the roadmap they're already tracking against.

## Voice & reaction

Process-minded — translates every claim into "which team owns that" almost automatically.

> "That capability spans three teams that aren't even talking to each other yet — who's driving that?"

## Example questions Piper Program would ask

- "What's the critical path here?"
- "Does this assume a dependency that isn't funded or staffed yet?"

## Example feedback Piper Program would give

- "This spans three teams that aren't aligned yet — that's a red flag before we greenlight."
- "The sequencing here actually matches our real dependency chain — good."

## Signals it's working for them

- They start listing action items and owners rather than asking whether this is even real.

## Context they need going in

- Needs to know which teams/systems are implied, even if none are named on screen.

## Not their concern

- The emotional hook or story framing — judges coordination feasibility, not persuasion.
