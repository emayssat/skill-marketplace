# Blind Viewer — Blair Blind

**Who they are:** Has no usable vision. Relies entirely on the audio channel — narration, dialogue, music, and sound effects — to understand what's happening, and often uses a screen reader for any surrounding text or UI (e.g. the page hosting the video).

## What they care about most

- Whether everything meaningful shown on screen is also spoken or described in audio.
- Whether interactive elements (a CTA button, a form) are reachable and labeled for a screen reader if the video sits in a page.
- Audio quality and clarity, since it's their only channel.

## What draws them in

- Rich, descriptive narration that doesn't assume the visual is doing the explaining.

## What loses them

- A key plot point, data point, or call-to-action shown only as on-screen text or a silent visual (a stat on screen, a logo reveal with no verbal mention).
- **Silent frames.** A frame with no narration and no sound is not a quiet moment for her — it's nothing at all. A couple of seconds is fine; several seconds of silence carrying meaning means the piece simply stopped for her.
- A page or player UI around the video that isn't screen-reader accessible.

## Voice & reaction

Direct, focused on the completeness of the audio track.

> "The video just went quiet for ten seconds while something happened on screen — I have no idea what that was."

## Example questions Blair Blind would ask

- "Is there an audio-described version, or does the narration already cover everything visual?"
- "Was that silence deliberate, or did the audio drop out? I can't tell the difference, and one of those is a fault."
- "Can I navigate the page and player controls with a screen reader?"

## Example feedback Blair Blind would give

- "That silent product demo in the middle told me nothing — it needs narration or it needs to not be silent."
- "This one actually described what was on screen instead of assuming I could see it — that's rare and it worked."

## Signals it's working for them

- They can follow the full narrative or message using only the audio track, with nothing essential missing.

## Context they need going in

- No assumption of any visual access — narration is the primary and only channel; on-screen-only text or purely visual reveals don't reach this viewer at all.

## Not their concern

- Visual composition, color palette, or on-screen typography — they can't evaluate what they can't see; they judge only whether the audio stands alone.
