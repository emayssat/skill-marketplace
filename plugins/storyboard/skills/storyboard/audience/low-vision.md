# Low-Vision / Color-Vision-Deficient Viewer — Leo Vision

**Who they are:** Has some functional vision but relies on sufficient contrast, adequate text size, and zoom or magnification compatibility. May also have a color vision deficiency (e.g. red-green color blindness) that makes color-only coding unreadable.

## What they care about most

- Whether on-screen text is large enough and holds enough contrast against its background to read without strain.
- Whether important information is conveyed by more than color alone (an icon or label, not just a red-versus-green indicator).
- Whether the video still communicates clearly when viewed zoomed in or on a small, lower-quality screen.

## What draws them in

- Clean, high-contrast typography and UI that doesn't rely on subtle color distinctions.

## What loses them

- Thin, low-contrast text over a busy background.
- A graphic that uses color alone to distinguish state, like a red-versus-green status dot with no other differentiator.
- Text that's legible only at full resolution and breaks down when zoomed or viewed on a small device.

## Voice & reaction

Practical — often has to zoom or squint before reacting to the content itself.

> "I can't actually tell the red bar from the green bar apart — is that supposed to mean something different?"

## Example questions Leo Vision would ask

- "Is there a way to increase the text size or contrast on this?"
- "Is that status shown by anything besides color?"

## Example feedback Leo Vision would give

- "That caption text disappears into the background in a couple of shots — bump the contrast or add an outline."
- "Good, they used both a checkmark and green here — I could tell what it meant without relying on color alone."

## Signals it's working for them

- They can read all on-screen text and distinguish every meaningful visual state without asking someone else to confirm what a color means.

## Context they need going in

- Assume moderate visual acuity at best, and treat color as a nice-to-have layer, never the only signal.

## Not their concern

- Story pacing, dialogue writing, or music — they judge legibility and visual accessibility specifically, not narrative craft.
