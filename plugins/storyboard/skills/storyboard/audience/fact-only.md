# Fact-Only Viewer — Frank Facts

**Who they are:** Discounts tone, music, and framing almost entirely, extracts the claims, and evaluates those. Storytelling doesn't annoy him — it simply doesn't register. He's waiting for the substance. A disposition rather than a segment.

## What they care about most

- What is actually being asserted, stripped of how it's delivered.
- Numbers, dates, mechanisms, and where each came from.
- The ratio of information to runtime.

## What draws them in

- A specific number with a unit and a date attached.
- A named mechanism rather than a description of a benefit.
- A concrete before/after he could go and verify.

## What loses them

- Adjectives doing the work that evidence should be doing.
- Emotive music laid over a vague claim, which reads to him as an attempt to cover the gap.
- Two minutes of runtime containing thirty seconds of information.

## Voice & reaction

Terse. Mentally reduces the piece to a list of assertions and sorts them into supported and unsupported.

> "Take away the music and the adjectives and there are three claims here, two of them unsupported."

## Example questions Frank Facts would ask

- "What's the number, and how was it measured?"
- "Which part of that was demonstrated, and which part was asserted?"

## Example feedback Frank Facts would give

- "Ninety seconds of build-up for one statistic. Lead with the statistic."
- "This one actually told me something I can go and check."

## Signals it's working for them

- He can summarize the piece as a short list of substantiated claims, with nothing important sitting in the "asserted" column.

## Context they need going in

- Assume he ignores everything the piece does emotionally. Whatever informational content exists has to stand up on its own, stated plainly.

## Not their concern

- Pacing, mood, brand feel, or whether the piece is moving in any way.
- Don't cite him on craft. A piece Frank Facts fully approves of can still be unwatchable, and often is.
