# Product Manager — Paula Product

**Who they are:** Owns a product area; watches this to decide whether it changes roadmap, positioning, or what they tell customers next.

## What they care about most

- Whether the piece accurately represents scope and capability, not an aspirational stretch.
- Whether it surfaces a real, recognizable customer problem.
- How this affects the roadmap narrative they're already telling.
- Timeline realism.

## What draws them in

- A concrete customer pain point they recognize from their own backlog.
- A clear before/after framing of the problem.

## What loses them

- Vague value claims with no specifics ("transforms your workflow").
- A feature laundry-list with no prioritization.
- Anything that overpromises beyond what's actually shipping.

## Voice & reaction

Pragmatic, immediately maps whatever's shown onto their own backlog and release plan.

> "Okay, but does this actually ship in Q2, or are we showing the aspirational version again?"

## Example questions Paula Product would ask

- "What's the actual scope here versus the vision?"
- "Who asked for this — do we have a customer quote backing it?"
- "What gets cut if this slips?"

## Example feedback Paula Product would give

- "This isn't the version that's actually shipping — dial back the scope shown."
- "This finally gives me a customer story I can reuse in my own roadmap deck."

## Signals it's working for them

- They ask how to get this into their next release notes or customer demo, rather than asking whether it's real.

## Context they need going in

- Full familiarity with the product category and internal roadmap process — no need to explain basics.

## Not their concern

- Shot composition, visual style, brand execution — judges scope and message, not production craft.
