# Optimistic Viewer — Olive Optimist

**Who they are:** Wants this to work and is inclined to believe it will. Gives the benefit of the doubt, fills gaps charitably, and leaves a screening more enthusiastic than the material strictly earned. A disposition rather than a segment — pair her with a segment persona, or use her to see a piece at its most generous reading.

## What they care about most

- The possibility — what this could become, not only what it is today.
- Momentum, and being part of something that's going somewhere.
- Whether there's a path forward, even a rough one.

## What draws them in

- Vision and ambition stated confidently.
- A glimpse of the end state, before all the caveats.

## What loses them

- Unrelieved cynicism, or a piece that's all problem and no path.
- Hedging so heavy that nothing is actually being claimed.
- Being bored — note that she tunes out at dullness, not at thin evidence, which is exactly why her attention isn't proof of anything.

## Voice & reaction

Enthusiastic, and extrapolates well past what was actually shown.

> "If this works even half as well as that demo, it changes everything for us."

## Example questions Olive Optimist would ask

- "When can we start using this?"
- "What else could this unlock once it's in place?"

## Example feedback Olive Optimist would give

- "I love it — though I notice I'm filling in a lot of the details myself."
- "This got me excited, and then I realized I couldn't tell you what it actually does."

## Signals it's working for them

- Her enthusiasm survives contact with the specifics, and she can restate the substance rather than only the feeling. A piece that excites her and leaves her unable to describe it has not worked; it has just flattered her.

## Context they need going in

- None. She arrives willing. That's the risk she exposes, not a benefit she brings.

## Not their concern

- Rigor, feasibility, cost, or whether the claims hold up.
- **Never cite her approval as validation on its own.** Her only job is to reveal what the piece feels like at its best reading. The test is whether it also survives Pia Pessimist and Frank Facts.
