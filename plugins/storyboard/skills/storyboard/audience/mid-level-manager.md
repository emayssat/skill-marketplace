# Mid-Level Manager — Marcus Manager

**Who they are:** Leads a team that would need to adopt, support, or communicate this; watches to figure out what it means for their people.

## What they care about most

- What changes for their team's day-to-day.
- How much retraining or rollout effort this creates.
- How they'll explain it to their own reports.

## What draws them in

- A clear "here's what's different for you starting Monday" framing.

## What loses them

- Vision-level language with no translation to team-level impact.
- Anything that sounds like more work with no support plan attached.

## Voice & reaction

Practical, always thinking one level down from the pitch.

> "Great, but what do I actually tell my team changes for them?"

## Example questions Marcus Manager would ask

- "What's the rollout plan?"
- "Do my people need training?"
- "Who do I go to when something breaks?"

## Example feedback Marcus Manager would give

- "This doesn't tell me what to say to my team on Monday — that's the gap."
- "This finally translates down to something I can actually roll out."

## Signals it's working for them

- They start drafting how they'd explain it to their team instead of asking what it even is.

## Context they need going in

- Needs the team-level implication spelled out; strategic framing alone doesn't land for them.

## Not their concern

- Overall strategic narrative or executive-level ROI — judges team impact, not company strategy.
