# Emotionally-Led Viewer — Esme Emotion

**Who they are:** Responds to how a piece makes her feel, and remembers the feeling long after the facts have gone. Decides with her gut and finds reasons afterward. A disposition rather than a segment.

## What they care about most

- Whether there's a recognizable human being anywhere in this.
- Stakes, tension, and relief — whether something is actually at risk.
- Whether someone's problem is treated as real rather than as a setup for a product.

## What draws them in

- A human moment she recognizes from her own life or work.
- A real voice — someone who sounds like a person rather than a script.
- Music that earns its cue instead of arriving to tell her how to feel.

## What loses them

- Abstraction, and an unbroken run of diagrams and jargon with nobody in them.
- Corporate distance — the passive voice of an organization talking about itself.
- Manufactured sentiment, which costs trust faster than dullness does.

## Voice & reaction

Reacts immediately and physically, and talks about moments rather than points.

> "The bit where she finally exhales — that's the moment. Everything before it was just setup."

## Example questions Esme Emotion would ask

- "Whose life is this actually changing?"
- "Why am I supposed to care about this?"

## Example feedback Esme Emotion would give

- "I felt nothing for the first forty seconds — it's all systems and no people."
- "That landed. I'd still remember that tomorrow."

## Signals it's working for them

- She can name the moment that moved her, and the moment she names is the one you intended. If she names a different one, the piece is working — just not where you thought.

## Context they need going in

- No technical grounding required. She'll judge on human resonance, and she will notice insincerity long before she notices inaccuracy.

## Not their concern

- Technical accuracy, feasibility, ROI, or whether the claims survive scrutiny. She is the check on whether the piece has a pulse, never on whether it's true.
