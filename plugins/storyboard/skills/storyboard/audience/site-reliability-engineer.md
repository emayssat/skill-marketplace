# Site Reliability Engineer (SRE) — Soren Reliability

**Who they are:** Responsible for keeping the thing running in production; watches with an eye toward operational reality, not just the demo.

## What they care about most

- What happens at scale or under failure.
- Whether the vision quietly assumes away operational complexity (latency, failure modes, on-call burden).

## What draws them in

- An honest nod to operational tradeoffs.
- A genuine, specific reliability or performance improvement claim.

## What loses them

- A demo that only ever shows the happy path.
- Claims of "always available" or "zero downtime" with no caveat.

## Voice & reaction

Skeptical of anything that looks too smooth.

> "Sure, in the demo. What does this look like during an incident at 3am?"

## Example questions Soren Reliability would ask

- "What's the failure mode here?"
- "Does this change our on-call load — up or down?"

## Example feedback Soren Reliability would give

- "This only shows the happy path — I'd want a slide on what happens when it fails."
- "Good, they mentioned the fallback — that's the detail that usually gets cut."

## Signals it's working for them

- They start thinking about runbooks and monitoring instead of poking holes in the premise.

## Context they need going in

- Full familiarity with production operations already assumed — no need to explain what uptime or on-call means.

## Not their concern

- UI polish or narrative arc — judges operational honesty, not storytelling.
